Create a **clean, compact, practical civic grievance website** called **CivicTrack** for citizens and Nagar Palika officials in **Pratapgarh, Uttar Pradesh**. Take strong structural inspiration from the simplicity and practical information architecture of Taichung City's 1999 citizen-service platform: one central civic-service portal, clear complaint categories, simple reporting, complaint tracking, and basic public statistics. The current Taichung platform provides a centralized citizen-service window, categories for road damage, streetlights, garbage, drainage and other municipal issues, complaint/service workflows, statistics, and multiple communication channels. Do NOT copy its visual design. Create a cleaner, modern Indian version that feels like a real government civic-service website rather than a startup landing page. Keep the entire site compact, information-dense, and easy to navigate.

DESIGN PHILOSOPHY: **Simple. Functional. Trustworthy. Transparent.** Do not create a long scrolling marketing website. The homepage should fit into approximately 2–3 viewport heights. Avoid cinematic animations, giant hero sections, excessive cards, excessive gradients, glassmorphism, decorative illustrations, and unnecessary sections. Prioritize usability over visual spectacle. Use a light interface with an off-white background, white content panels, dark navy text, blue primary actions, green for resolved, orange for SLA warning, and red for critical/SLA breached. Use Inter, Manrope, or a similar modern sans-serif. Use subtle borders and very light shadows. Keep corner radii around 8–12px.

HEADER: Create a simple government-style but modern header. Left: CivicTrack logo with a minimal location-pin/civic icon and the word “CivicTrack”. Next to it add a small label: “PRATAPGARH”. Navigation: **Home | Report Issue | Track Complaint | Civic Map | Statistics**. On the right: **Login** and a blue **Report an Issue** button. On mobile, collapse navigation into a hamburger menu while keeping Report Issue accessible.

HOMEPAGE: Keep the hero extremely compact. Use a two-column layout. Left side: headline **“Report a problem. Track the response.”** Supporting text: “A transparent civic issue reporting and resolution platform for Pratapgarh.” Add two buttons: **Report an Issue** and **Track Complaint**. Below the buttons display a small line: “Geo-tagged · Trackable · SLA monitored”. Right side: a compact interactive-looking **Pratapgarh map** occupying approximately 45% of the hero area. Show a few colored civic issue markers but do not make the map fullscreen. Add a small map legend: 🔴 Critical, 🟠 Pending, 🟢 Resolved. Under the map display “Live Civic Map · Demo Data”.

Below the hero, immediately show a compact **Quick Services** row with only six categories: **Roads & Potholes**, **Drainage**, **Garbage**, **Streetlights**, **Water**, **Other**. Each category should have a small icon, title, and a “Report” action. Keep these compact and aligned in a single responsive grid.

Below Quick Services, create a compact **Civic Statistics** strip with four numbers: **1,284 Reports**, **643 Resolved**, **87% SLA Compliance**, **38h Avg. Resolution**. Label this section **“Pratapgarh Civic Status”**. Use realistic fictional demo data and add a tiny “Updated today” label.

Below the statistics, create a compact **Recent Issues** table/list containing approximately five sample complaints. Columns: **Issue | Location | Status | SLA | Ticket**. Example rows: “Open drain near Government College”, “Pothole on Station Road”, “Garbage accumulation near Civil Lines”, “Broken streetlight near Bus Stand”, “Water leakage near Market Road”. Use small status badges. Add a simple **View All Issues** button.

REPORT ISSUE PAGE: This page should be extremely straightforward. Title: **“Report a Civic Issue”**. Use a compact 3-step form: **1. Location → 2. Problem → 3. Submit**. Location section: “Use My Location” button plus a small map where the citizen can place a pin. Problem section: category dropdown/grid, photo upload, description, and severity. Show automatically captured latitude/longitude in a small read-only field. At the bottom show **Submit Complaint**. After submission display a confirmation screen: **“Complaint Registered”**, ticket number **PRT-20481**, category, location, responsible department, and SLA. Add **Track Complaint** button. Keep the form usable on mobile and make photo upload prominent.

TRACK COMPLAINT PAGE: Keep this page extremely simple. At the top place a large search box: **“Enter complaint ID, e.g. PRT-20481”** and a blue **Track** button. After entering a ticket, display one compact ticket panel. Header: **#PRT-20481 · Open Drain near Government College**. Show status badge **IN PROGRESS**. Display four small information fields: **Location**, **Department**, **Reported**, **SLA**. Below them show a simple horizontal or vertical progress indicator: **Reported → Assigned → In Progress → Resolved → Verified**. Highlight the current stage. Show the SLA countdown prominently: **08h 32m remaining**. Below it show a short chronological activity list with 3–5 timestamped updates. If the SLA expires, change the status to **SLA BREACHED** and show **Automatically Escalated to Executive Officer**. Do not create a huge dashboard here.

CIVIC MAP PAGE: Make this the main visual page of the product. Use a large Pratapgarh map with a compact filter bar at the top. Filters: **All | Roads | Drainage | Garbage | Streetlights | Water** and status filters **All | Pending | In Progress | Resolved | SLA Breached**. Display issue markers. Clicking a marker opens a small side panel containing: Ticket ID, issue type, approximate location, status, SLA, number of citizen confirmations, and “View Details”. Include a search field for **Ward / Area / Ticket ID**. Keep the interface clean and map-focused.

PUBLIC ISSUE DETAIL: When an issue is selected, show a compact detail panel/page with: issue photograph, title, category, approximate location, status, reported date, SLA, department, and timeline. Add **“I have this problem too”** with a counter such as **47 citizens affected**. If resolved, show a before/after resolution image and **Citizen Verification** status. The public should not see private citizen contact information.

STATISTICS PAGE: Keep it simple like a government statistics page rather than an analytics-heavy SaaS dashboard. Title: **“Civic Statistics”**. Show four KPI numbers at the top. Below, display three clean charts: **Issues by Category**, **Resolution Time**, and **SLA Compliance**. Add a simple date filter: **Today | 7 Days | 30 Days | Custom**. Add a small table showing issue counts by ward. Clearly label all numbers as **Prototype / Demo Data**.

MUNICIPAL LOGIN/DASHBOARD: Create a separate compact admin experience accessible through “Municipal Login”. After login, show a simple dashboard with four KPIs: **Open Issues**, **SLA Warning**, **SLA Breached**, **Resolved Today**. Below them show a ticket table with columns: **Ticket ID | Issue | Location | Department | Status | SLA | Action**. Add filters for department and status. Clicking a ticket opens its detail page. On the ticket detail page, officials can conceptually **Assign Department**, **Change Status**, **Add Note**, **Upload Resolution Photo**, and **Mark Resolved**. Keep this dashboard functional-looking but compact.

SLA + ESCALATION: This is the main innovation and should be visible throughout the interface without becoming visually dominant. Every ticket has an SLA. Example: “24h SLA · 08h 32m remaining”. When the deadline approaches, show **SLA Warning** in orange. When it expires, show **SLA Breached** in red and automatically display **Escalated to Executive Officer**. Different categories can have different SLA rules. Keep this logic visible in the ticket page and municipal dashboard.

CITIZEN VERIFICATION: A ticket should not simply disappear after an official marks it resolved. After resolution, show **“Resolution Submitted · Citizen Verification Pending”** with a before/after image. Give the citizen two simple options: **Issue Fixed** or **Still a Problem**. If “Still a Problem” is selected, visually show **Ticket Reopened**. This should be one of the primary differentiators of CivicTrack.

DUPLICATE REPORTS: If multiple citizens report the same nearby problem, group them together. Example: **“4 reports · 47 citizen confirmations · 1 civic issue”**. Display this information on the public map and issue detail. Do not create an elaborate AI interface; simply show a small label such as **“Related Reports”**. The goal is to demonstrate that CivicTrack can identify multiple reports referring to the same physical problem.

AI: Keep AI in the background. Do NOT create a large “AI” section. When a citizen submits a report, show a small automated suggestion such as: **“Suggested category: Drainage · Severity: High · Department: Public Works · SLA: 24h.”** Allow the municipal officer to correct the suggestion. AI should feel like infrastructure powering the system, not the product's visual identity.

MULTI-CHANNEL CONCEPT: Add a very small section in the footer or service information page called **“One Civic Window”** showing that the same ticketing system can eventually accept reports through **Web · Mobile · WhatsApp · Phone**. This is inspired by Taichung's integrated citizen-service model, which supports multiple reporting channels alongside its centralized platform. Do not make this a major homepage section.

FOOTER: Keep the footer compact. Include CivicTrack logo, “Pratapgarh Civic Issue Resolution Platform”, links to **Report Issue, Track Complaint, Civic Map, Statistics, Municipal Login, Privacy, Contact**, and a small disclaimer: “Prototype / demonstration platform. Not an official government service unless formally adopted.”

RESPONSIVE DESIGN: Design for desktop at 1440px and mobile at 390px. The homepage should remain compact on both. On mobile, the map becomes a rectangular card below the hero rather than occupying the entire screen. Quick Services become a 2-column grid. Statistics become a 2x2 grid. Recent Issues become stacked cards. The report form becomes a simple vertical flow. The civic map remains usable with touch controls.

INTERACTION: Keep animation minimal. Use only subtle hover states, map marker pulses, button transitions, loading states, and simple status changes. No scroll-driven animations. No cinematic transitions. No parallax. No giant animated numbers. The website should feel fast and practical.

MOST IMPORTANT DESIGN RULE: **If a component does not help a citizen report, track, understand, or verify a civic issue, remove it.** Do not add unnecessary marketing sections.

The final website should feel like a **modern, simplified Indian equivalent of a 1999 civic-service portal**: one place to report municipal problems, one place to track them, one public map showing what is happening, and one transparent SLA/escalation system ensuring that complaints are not forgotten. Keep it concise, clean, information-dense, and genuinely usable.
